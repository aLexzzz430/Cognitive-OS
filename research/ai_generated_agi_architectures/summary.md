# What the eleven proposals actually specify

The strongest disagreement appears when a restart happens halfway through a move. Qwen calls actions idempotent but leaves implementation to the tool. Codex places the effect and its receipt in the same transaction. RWKV saves at the end of an episode, while Gemma saves the plan stack. An episode checkpoint can predate the move, and a saved plan can still point to the old room. The comparison therefore asks what a restart can prove about an already-issued command. Logging more events only helps if the adapter's outcome can be recovered. [Qwen](raw_outputs/qwen.txt) · [Codex](raw_outputs/codex.txt) · [RWKV](raw_outputs/rwkv.txt) · [Gemma](raw_outputs/gemma.txt)

A late scan creates a different ambiguity. Qwen gives a newer event precedence over an older one. Codex retains conflicts and requests a fresh inspection. LFM updates graph beliefs and replans. Cydonia retains competing event branches before pruning them. These choices lead to different behavior when two sensors disagree. Confidence updating needs a model of sensor reliability. Version ordering works within one authoritative stream. A second sensor with its own counter introduces an unresolved comparison. [LFM](raw_outputs/lfm.txt) · [Cydonia](raw_outputs/cydonia.txt)

The planning suggestions range from Monte Carlo tree search to hierarchical task networks and heuristic search. In two rooms, a single deterministic transfer gives little opportunity to test search quality. Rocinante does offer a measurable breadth-versus-depth choice for a larger warehouse. Magnum's comparison removes both safety and coordination from its baseline, which would make an observed difference hard to attribute. Llama suggests a robotic arm and 3D scene, neither of which the offline ledger requires. SmolLM2 names a human-guided comparator, but the amount of human information supplied would need to be matched. [Rocinante](raw_outputs/rocinante.txt) · [Magnum](raw_outputs/magnum.txt) · [Llama](raw_outputs/llama.txt) · [SmolLM2](raw_outputs/smollm.txt)

Most proposals make one agent sufficient for this sequential task. They differ on when a person should intervene. RWKV asks before a critical move, Qwen uses cost or confidence, and Codex ties approval to a change of destination. A choice here can be tested as the rate of unnecessary interruptions at a fixed number of incorrect releases. A plan that pauses on every move may otherwise look safe simply because it does little.

Several responses write down numerical baselines or targets, including Rocinante's 47 steps and Stheno's random-search timing. No model executed those experiments in this collection. The only measured results are the four fixture counts reported in the synthesis. The task is deliberately small, there is one recorded proposal per system, sampling controls differ and three endings are incomplete. These records support a comparison of specific design suggestions. A performance ranking would require implementations and independently held-out tasks.
